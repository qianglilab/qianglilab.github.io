Organoid Intelligence for Translational Medicine

Our research program aims to develop next-generation bioelectronic-integrated organoids to advance our understanding of human tissue development, elucidate disease mechanisms, and enable translational medicine. By integrating human stem cell-derived organoids with flexible nanoelectronics, spatial transcriptomics, multi-omics, and artificial intelligence (AI), we seek to uncover and engineer the molecular and electrophysiological processes that govern organoid development and functional maturation. Ultimately, our goal is to establish more physiologically relevant human models for disease research, therapeutic discovery, and regenerative medicine.

Despite significant advances in human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived organoids, incomplete functional maturation, cellular heterogeneity, and limited recapitulation of native tissue architecture and function remain major challenges. Furthermore, the interplay between molecular programs and electrophysiological activities during organoid development and maturation remains poorly understood. Our research seeks to address the following fundamental questions:

01 Organoid Intelligence

We integrate human stem cell-derived organoids with flexible bioelectronics and AI-assisted modeling to monitor, interact with, and stimulate developing organoids. This approach aims to promote functional maturation and establish intelligent organoid platforms with real-time sensing and adaptive control capabilities.

Integrating human organoids, flexible bioelectronics, and AI-assisted modeling to advance organoid intelligence.

Research question: How can flexible bioelectronics and AI-assisted modeling be integrated to monitor, interact with, and stimulate developing organoids, thereby promoting their functional maturation?

02 Bioelectronics and Gene-to-Function Mapping

We combine multimodal bioelectronic recording and stimulation with spatial transcriptomics and molecular profiling to investigate the relationships between gene expression programs and electrophysiological activities. By integrating molecular and functional measurements, we aim to understand how cellular and tissue functions emerge during organoid development and maturation.

Connecting molecular programs with electrophysiological activities through bioelectronics and spatial transcriptomics.

Research question: How are single-cell gene expression programs linked to electrophysiological activities during organoid development, maturation, and disease progression?

03 Human Disease Modeling

We engineer bioelectronic-integrated human organoids to investigate disease mechanisms and identify potential therapeutic strategies. Our research explores neurological diseases, including Parkinson's disease, as well as cardiovascular diseases and metabolic disorders such as diabetes.

Modeling Parkinson's disease using human organoids and integrated bioelectronic approaches.

Research question: To what extent can bioelectronic-integrated organoids recapitulate human diseases, including neurodegenerative disorders, cardiovascular diseases, and diabetes, to reveal underlying disease mechanisms and identify potential therapeutic strategies?

04 Translational Organoid Medicine

We aim to develop mature and intelligent organoid platforms that integrate real-time monitoring, bioelectronic modulation, and AI-driven analysis. By combining in vitro human organoid systems with in vivo mouse models, we seek to advance disease modeling, preclinical evaluation, therapeutic discovery, and regenerative medicine.

Integrating intelligent organoids, bioelectronics, and in vivo mouse models to advance translational medicine.

Research question: Can we engineer mature, intelligent organoids capable of real-time, closed-loop monitoring and modulation of biological processes to advance disease modeling, therapeutic discovery, and regenerative medicine?

To address these questions, we employ interdisciplinary approaches spanning stem cell biology, organoid engineering, flexible nanoelectronics, electrophysiology, spatial transcriptomics, advanced microscopy, molecular biology, and computational modeling. Our long-term vision is to integrate stem cell-derived organoids, multimodal bioelectronics, single-cell spatial omics, and AI-driven modeling to characterize, monitor, and engineer organoid development and functional maturation under physiological and pathological conditions. Through this integrated approach, we aim to bridge fundamental discoveries in human biology with innovative bioengineered platforms for disease modeling, therapeutic discovery, and translational medicine.
